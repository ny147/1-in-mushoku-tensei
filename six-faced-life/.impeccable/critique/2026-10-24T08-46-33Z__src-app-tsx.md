# Six-Faced Life UX/UI review — 2026-10-24

Scope: the current application rooted at `src/App.tsx`, including new-life setup, chronicle, playback, decisions, yearly focus, character/progression, world, help and archive. Review only; the following changes are recommendations, not implemented or approved behavior. The existing illustrated-chronicle direction remains appropriate.

## Overall assessment

The visual identity feels authored for this game: warm paper, forest ink, literary typography and original countryside imagery support a personal second-life story. The main opportunity is clearer interaction. Players should immediately understand what they can do, what their choice changed, and what will happen next.

The opening and birth record establish anticipation well. The first decision then loses momentum: it is replaced by a generic quiet state, while its actual outcome is farther down the chronicle. On mobile, small time controls and missing help make that learning gap harder to recover from.

## What works

- New-life setup has one obvious action and optional inputs. The birth record introduces the generated person before asking for a decision.
- Narrative choices are full-width and comfortably sized on mobile (299px wide, 63–79px high in the inspected state). Pending decisions explicitly stop advancement.
- The progression interface shows experience, skill and reputation requirements with words and values; it does not promise an immediate promotion merely because requirements are met. Pause/reload preserved the inspected choice and history.

## Priority issues

### 1. [P1] Choices and navigation lose the player's place

After a choice, its card disappears into “A little time to live.” The narrative outcome is stored below the yearly-focus panel, while stat changes lack a nearby before/after explanation. In one observed opening choice, Health moved 80→78 and magic experience 0→5. Keyboard focus returned to the web area when the activated choice disappeared. Navigating to Help left focus on the sidebar button rather than the new content.

**Recommendation:** Keep a short outcome in the decision area, show meaningful changes, and offer a clear continuation action. Move focus to an appropriate stable outcome/page heading and announce the short result. Preserve the full chronicle entry and avoid announcing every playback tick.

Evidence: `src/App.tsx:218` and `:230`; `src/components/LifeView.tsx:277`, `:306`, `:497`. Browser verified focus transitions and outcome placement; screen-reader impact is inferred, not NVDA-tested. Suggested workflow: `$impeccable clarify` and `$impeccable harden`.

### 2. [P1] Mobile help is missing, and Watch mode hides its consequence

“How to play” is inside `.sidebar-bottom`, which becomes `display:none` on phones. Opening the mobile menu exposes only the four main destinations. The Watch mode toggle has no nearby explanation that it makes irreversible story choices for the player. That explanation exists on the help page the phone menu cannot reach. The decision card still says “Time pauses while you decide” during automated play.

**Recommendation:** Keep How to play in mobile navigation. Add a plain nearby description such as “Makes choices for you while playing,” use explicit manual/automatic state text, and adapt the decision reassurance to the active mode. Keep detailed rules in help, with small contextual guidance at the controls.

Evidence: `src/App.tsx:309`, `:445`, `:651`; `src/styles.css:2657`; `src/components/LifeView.tsx:323`. Mobile missing-help path and labels browser verified. Automatic-choice timing/conflicting copy source verified; Watch mode automation was not exercised. Suggested workflow: `$impeccable clarify` and `$impeccable adapt`.

### 3. [P2] Phone utility controls are tiny, while the blocking choice starts below the fold

At 390×844, measured controls included Play/pause 100×14px, speed 27×24px, +1 year 46×22px, Next decision 108×26px, hamburger 34×34px, and the All history filter 10×21px. The scene, heading and time controls occupied the first viewport before any choice button. Mobile choice explanations are 10px; the pause reassurance is 9px.

**Recommendation:** Enlarge utility hit areas toward 44px without making the visual style heavy. Increase decision-support text to a readable size and shorten or collapse the scene during a pending decision so the next action is easier to find. Keep the original artwork and calm typography. The narrative choice buttons themselves already have useful hit areas.

Evidence: `src/styles.css:489`, `:508`, `:2739`, `:2765`, `:2812`. Browser verified measurements and phone fold; source verified font sizes. No horizontal overflow was observed in the inspected 390px layout. Suggested workflow: `$impeccable adapt`.

### 4. [P2] Training requirements explain the destination better than the next action

The interface shows experience and instruction gates, but “seek a mentor to progress” does not explain where instruction opportunities come from. Yearly focus, magic discipline, training rank and the full spellbook are separated. A new player can read the requirements without understanding which control changes deliberate practice or which opportunities arrive as story events.

**Recommendation:** Add one contextual next-step sentence beside the current goal: explain that yearly magic focus trains the selected discipline, that switching disciplines preserves prior learning, and that higher instruction comes through eligible story opportunities. Explain the currently unmet condition without promising a mentor or promotion immediately. Link the compact training section to its fuller spellbook view.

Evidence: `src/components/LifeView.tsx:154`, `:199`; `src/components/Progression.tsx:28`, `:65`, `:190`; `src/App.tsx:624`. Adult progression was inspected read-only, and source confirms the split controls. This is an expert-review recommendation; no player comprehension study was conducted. Suggested workflow: `$impeccable clarify`.

### 5. [P1] “Not saved” offers no recovery action

A storage write failure changes the status to “Not saved” but provides no persistent explanation or retry action. The tooltip merely explains that lives are stored in the browser. A player may continue without understanding whether the current progress will survive closing the page.

**Recommendation:** Provide a persistent explanation, a retry-save action, and a truthful success/failure result while preserving the current in-memory life. Do not recommend clearing browser data. Avoid claiming an ending is saved when persistence has failed.

Evidence: `src/App.tsx:131`, `:341`; `src/components/Archive.tsx:88`. Source-only failure-path finding; no storage failure was injected. Suggested workflow: `$impeccable harden`.

## Cognitive load and player journey

The main load comes from remembering values after a choice, interpreting several playback controls together, and connecting training requirements to the right action. The initial decision's three choices are manageable. Adult focus can expose eight priorities, and the world guide contains six regions; these bounded catalogues should be grouped clearly rather than arbitrarily cut down to four entries.

The opening/birth sequence builds ownership. The generic post-choice state weakens the emotional payoff; immediate narrative feedback would restore it. Ending source provides reflection, legacy and retained history, but no completed ending was browser-tested in this review.

Persona risks: a first-time player may miss the result of a choice or misunderstand Watch mode; a keyboard/assistive player loses context across screen changes; a distracted phone player has small utility targets and no visible Help destination. Existing explicit choice labels, native inputs, large story buttons and paused reload are useful foundations.

## Smaller observations

- “Begin a new chapter” in an empty archive returns to the active life when one exists; “Return to your life” would describe that action more accurately (`src/components/Archive.tsx:117`, `src/App.tsx:500`).
- “Your latest memory” is attached to ordinary work/choice entries, while Memories is also a distinct filter (`src/components/LifeView.tsx:391`). Use “Latest moment” for the generic label.
- The playback button's accessible name remains “Pause time” while its visible text is “Waiting for you.” Include pending-decision context without incorrectly changing the underlying playback semantics (`src/App.tsx:412`).
- A narrow-breakpoint map-label rule uses 5px text (`src/styles.css:3288`); inspect that specific map breakpoint before treating it as a verified rendering defect.

## Recommended first pass

**User-confirmed priority, October 24 follow-up:** “What to do next / time controls” was the most confusing part of play. Prioritize clear current state and next action, understandable time/automatic-choice controls, and visible decision outcomes. This records the user's priority; the specific interaction design still needs to be settled before implementation.

Start with the daily play loop: visible choice outcomes and keyboard continuity, reachable mobile help with clear Watch mode wording, and larger phone utility controls. Follow with actionable progression guidance and save-failure recovery. Preserve simulation balance, stable event identifiers, seeded behavior, existing lives and the approved visual identity.

Questions for the next design pass: Should a resolved choice stay visible until the player continues, or remain visible while timed play resumes? Which part most often caused confusion in actual play: advancing time, training progression, or finding information? These recommendations remain proposals until the user selects implementation scope.
